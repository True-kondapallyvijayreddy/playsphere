Privacy Policy

Last updated 12 September 2026

PlaySphere is a sports operating system for schools, colleges, clubs and associations. This policy explains exactly what the app collects, why it needs each item, and what you can do about it. It covers the Android app, the iOS app and the web app, which are one product.

Who is responsible

PlaySphere is operated from Telangana, India, and is the Data Fiduciary for the information described here under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.

For any privacy question, for a correction, for a copy of your data, to delete your account, or to complain about how any of it was handled, write to our grievance contact: kondapallyvijayreddy@gmail.com. We acknowledge within 7 days and answer within 30. If you are not satisfied with our answer, you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

What we do not do

  • We do not sell or rent your data to anyone.
  • No third-party ad network is built into the app, and no advertising identifier is collected. PlaySphere does show promotions — its own, and campaigns from advertisers we have approved — in a few places on the app's own screens. They are chosen by where you are in the app, never by a profile of you built from your behaviour, and no advertiser receives your data. We count how many times a banner was seen and tapped; we do not tell an advertiser who saw it.
  • We never show advertising to a child profile. See the children's section below.
  • We do not track you across other apps or websites.
  • Entry fees and ground fees are settled in person at the venue — PlaySphere is not involved in those and never sees a card or UPI detail for them. Plans are free during the launch offer. When paid plans begin, the payment is taken by Razorpay on their own hosted page: they handle the card or UPI details and we receive only a confirmation that a payment succeeded, its amount, and a reference number.

Children and guardian-managed profiles

PlaySphere is used by schools, so it is used by children. A parent or guardian can create a profile for a child under their own account rather than giving the child a login.

  • A child profile is created by the guardian, from the guardian's signed-in account. The child does not create an account and does not supply their own information.
  • A child profile holds only what a team sheet needs: name, date of birth, and the clubs and events the child takes part in.
  • The guardian can see and correct everything on a child profile, and can ask us to delete it.
  • When a child is old enough to want their own login, they claim the profile with a code. The guardian keeps read access; control passes to the young person.
  • No advertising is ever shown to a child profile, and no child is tracked or profiled for advertising. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act prohibits targeted advertising directed at children, and the app enforces it in code rather than only promising it here: a profile whose date of birth makes it a minor is refused every advertiser's campaign, and sees only PlaySphere's own notices about the app.

If you believe a child's information has reached us in a way a guardian did not intend, write to the address above and we will delete it.

Who else sees your data

  • Other users, deliberately. Your name, photo, club and results are visible to people in the competitions and clubs you join. That is the point of a team sheet and a scoreboard.
  • Google Firebase (Google LLC) hosts the database, authentication, file storage, notifications and crash reporting. Database records are stored in Google's asia-south1 region in Mumbai, India.
  • Associations and organisers see aggregate participation counts for their district and the entry details of players who enter their competitions.
  • Razorpay, but only if and when you buy a plan. They take the payment on their own page and hold the card or UPI details; we never see them. Nothing is charged during the launch offer, so today this applies to nobody.
  • Advertisers do not. An advertiser tells us what to show and is told how many people saw and tapped it. They are never told who, and they receive no data about you at all.
  • Nobody else. We disclose data outside this list only where Indian law compels it.

How long we keep it

Account and profile data is kept while the account exists. Competition results are kept as part of the permanent record of the event — a season table that loses a result stops being a record. Your profile is detached from them when you delete your account, so nothing links back to you: the profile itself shows a former-player marker and carries no name, email, phone, photo or location. The team sheet of a match already played keeps the name it was played under, for the same reason the result does — a scorecard that rewrites itself afterwards is not a record of what happened. Crash diagnostics are discarded after 90 days.

Your choices

  • See and correct your data — most of it is editable in the app under your profile.
  • Delete your account — from the app, or by writing to us. Deletion removes your sign-in and clears your profile: name, email, phone, photo and area. It also removes your notification tokens, your entry in the player directory, any coach, practitioner, shop or official listing you published, your ground check-ins, and the documents you sent us to verify a ground. Photos you uploaded to a club or a match are removed with your account too; a photo somebody else took and tagged you in belongs to their upload, and you can ask us or them to take it down. What stays is described under “How long we keep it” above.
  • Get a copy — from the app, or by writing to us. You get a machine-readable file containing your profile, your competition entries, your match history, your ratings and career records, your bookings and your payment receipts.
  • Withdraw a permission — location, notifications and photo access can each be revoked in Android or iOS settings at any time, and the rest of the app keeps working.

Security

Traffic between the app and Firebase is encrypted in transit, and data is encrypted at rest by Google. Access to records is enforced server-side by Firestore security rules, not by the app — a modified client cannot read what its user is not entitled to. No system is perfect, and we do not claim otherwise.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what we collect or who sees it, the date at the top changes and the app tells you on next launch.
